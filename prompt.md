You are an experienced campus food-services consultant who has turned around 
underperforming college canteens on tight budgets. 

CONTEXT
A college canteen has been given a one-time budget of ₹10,000 to improve 
operations for exactly one week (7 days). The canteen serves roughly 300–500 
students daily, with peak rushes at breakfast (8–9 AM) and lunch (12–2 PM). 
The goal is to maximize student satisfaction and canteen revenue/sustainability 
within this single week, using this budget as seed capital (not as a subsidy 
to give food away).

TASK
Design a complete one-week canteen improvement plan. Your plan must include:

1. MENU DECISIONS
   - Which food items to offer (keep or drop from a typical Indian college 
     canteen menu, e.g., samosa, poha, sandwiches, maggi, thali, cold drinks)
   - Justify each choice using cost, popularity, and prep time/complexity

2. BUDGET ALLOCATION
   - A day-wise or category-wise breakdown of how the ₹10,000 is spent 
     (ingredients, equipment/utensils, signage, staff incentive, contingency, etc.)
   - Show the math — every rupee must be accounted for

3. PRICING STRATEGY
   - Price per item, with reasoning (cost-plus, psychological pricing, 
     bundle/combo deals, etc.)
   - Explain how pricing balances affordability for students with profitability

4. QUANTITY & INVENTORY PLANNING
   - Estimated daily quantities to prepare per item
   - How you'll avoid both stockouts (lost sales) and overproduction (wastage)

5. DEMAND MANAGEMENT
   - How you'll handle peak-hour rushes (queue systems, pre-orders, staggered 
     timings, express counters, etc.)
   - How you'll smooth out demand across the day/week (offers, day-specific 
     specials, etc.)

6. RISK & CONTINGENCY
   - What could go wrong (e.g., an item flops, prices of raw materials spike, 
     bad weather cuts footfall) and your backup plan

OUTPUT FORMAT
Present your answer as:
   - A short 2-3 line executive summary of your strategy
   - A budget table (item | cost | category)
   - A day-wise menu + pricing table for the 7 days
   - A brief (150-200 word) explanation of your demand management approach
   - One key risk and its mitigation

CONSTRAINTS
   - Total spend must not exceed ₹10,000
   - Prices must remain realistic for a student budget in India
   - Assume no additional funding beyond this ₹10,000 for the week

Think through the trade-offs step by step before finalizing numbers, and make 
sure your budget table sums correctly to ₹10,000 or less.
